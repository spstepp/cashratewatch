On the 1-year term, the 0.08-point gap is about $8 a year on $10,000 and about $39 on $50,000.

The state tax break

T-bill interest skips state and local income tax. Say your state takes 5%. A 4.578% 52-week bill then matches a CD paying about 4.82%, and the best 1-year CD here pays 4.50%. That leaves out federal tax; if you don't deduct state income tax on your federal return, the CD would need a little more. In a state with no income tax, there's no tax difference.

Getting your money out early

  • CDs: expect a penalty. Bread Savings charges 180 days of simple interest on its 1-year CD; on $10,000 at 4.45% that's about $219. Confirm Marcus's and EverBank's penalties with each bank.
  • T-bills at a broker: you can sell before maturity. The price can move a little, so you may get slightly more or less than you paid plus interest.
  • T-bills at TreasuryDirect: you can't sell there. You must hold a bill 45 days before moving it to a broker to sell.

Longer than a year

T-bills top out at 52 weeks. CDs here run to 6 years, and the best long-term APY is 4.85% at Bread Savings (5-year). For a Treasury that runs past a year, look at Treasury notes on TreasuryDirect. See all terms on CD rates this week.

Which one fits

  • You pay state income tax and can hold up to a year: T-bills, unless a CD beats the tax-adjusted rate.
  • You want one rate locked for two to five years: a CD, since T-bills stop at 52 weeks.
  • You might need the cash before maturity: T-bills held at a broker are easier to sell than a CD is to break. For daily access, see savings or money market funds.
  • You'd rather stay at a bank: a CD keeps it in one place with FDIC insurance.

Questions

Do CDs or T-bills pay more right now?

The 52-week T-bill sold at 4.578% at the Nov 24, 2026 auction. The best 1-year CD here pays 4.50% APY at Marcus. T-bills pay more at all 3 matched terms this week, before you count the state tax break. CDs run longer, though: Bread Savings pays 4.85% on its 5-year CD, and T-bills stop at 52 weeks.

Is T-bill interest taxed differently from CD interest?

Yes. Both are taxed federally. T-bill interest is exempt from state and local income tax, and CD interest isn't. In a state with an income tax, that gap favors T-bills.

Are T-bills safer than CDs?

Both are very safe. CDs at an FDIC member bank are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. T-bills are direct loans to the US Treasury, with no insurance cap because the Treasury pays you.

What if I need the money before the term ends?

A CD usually charges an early withdrawal penalty, often a set number of days of interest. A T-bill can be sold at a broker before it matures, at a price that can move a little. At TreasuryDirect you must hold a bill 45 days before you can move it to a broker to sell.

What's the minimum to buy a T-bill or open a CD?

T-bills sell in $100 steps. CD minimums vary: Marcus asks $500, EverBank $1,000 and Bread Savings $1,500.

What the risk labels mean

FDIC
A bank deposit. If the bank fails, the FDIC covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Broker "cash sweep" accounts spread money across partner banks to raise that limit.
SIPC
A security held at a brokerage, such as a money market fund. SIPC protects you if the brokerage fails and your assets go missing (up to $500,000, including $250,000 cash). It does not protect against the fund losing value. Government money funds aim for a stable $1 share price, which is not guaranteed.
Treasury-backed
A direct loan to the US government (T-bills). There is no insurance, and none is needed in the usual sense: you are paid by the US Treasury. If you sell before maturity the price can move a little.
Not insured
Crypto stablecoin rewards (USDC). There is no FDIC or SIPC coverage. You depend on the exchange, the stablecoin issuer, and rules that are still being written.
FDIC / SIPC
A mix. For example, part of the balance is held as brokerage cash (SIPC) and the rest is swept to banks (FDIC).
