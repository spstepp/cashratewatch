Interactive Brokers vs Schwab: who pays more on cash?

Interactive Brokers pays 3.38% on uninvested cash and Schwab's default Bank Sweep pays 0.01% APY. The IBKR rate comes with strings: no interest on the first $10,000, and the full rate only if your account is worth more than $100,000.

The Schwab fix: buy a money fund

Schwab's own cash page points savers to a money fund such as SWVXX, which yielded 3.78% (7-day yield as of Nov 23, 2026). Schwab says it can be bought in a single trade with no minimum and no transaction fee (its systems need at least $1 per trade), with next-business-day access when you sell. It isn't automatic: new deposits and sale proceeds land back in the sweep. SWVXX is a prime fund, not FDIC-insured, and covered by SIPC only for custody. The full walk-through is on Schwab bank sweep vs SWVXX.

The fine print at IBKR

  • Interest is paid on settled cash only.
  • The $10,000 threshold applies to each currency you hold, and balances in separate IBKR accounts aren't combined.
  • The rate is tied to a benchmark, so it moves when short-term rates move. IBKR can change it at any time.
  • If your account was opened through another broker, that broker sets your rate, and it may pay less or nothing.

Which one fits

  • You keep well over $10,000 in cash and your account tops $100,000: IBKR pays the higher rate on cash without buying anything.
  • You keep less than $10,000 in cash: IBKR pays nothing on it, and Schwab's sweep pays almost nothing. A money fund or a high-yield savings account will do more.
  • You're happy at Schwab: buy SWVXX or another Schwab money fund. At 3.78%, it yields more than IBKR's 3.38% on cash this week.
  • You want every broker side by side: see best broker cash rates.

Questions

Does Interactive Brokers or Schwab pay more on cash?

Interactive Brokers, by a wide margin, if your cash is above $10,000: 3.38% vs 0.01% on Schwab's default sweep this week. At Schwab, a money fund such as SWVXX yielded 3.78%, so buying one closes the gap.

Does Interactive Brokers pay interest on the first $10,000?

No. IBKR pays no interest on the first $10,000 of uninvested USD cash. Interest is paid only on the settled cash above that, so your blended rate is lower than the headline rate.

Why is my IBKR interest rate lower than the advertised rate?

Two reasons. The first $10,000 earns nothing, and the full 3.38% applies only to accounts with a net asset value above $100,000. Smaller accounts get a lower rate that rises as the account nears $100,000. IBKR Lite pays less than IBKR Pro. IBKR's interest page has a calculator for your exact rate.

What does Schwab pay on uninvested cash?

0.01% APY this week, whether the cash sits in the Bank Sweep or the Schwab One Interest feature. Schwab suggests a money fund such as SWVXX for cash you don't plan to spend soon.

What the risk labels mean

FDIC
A bank deposit. If the bank fails, the FDIC covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Broker "cash sweep" accounts spread money across partner banks to raise that limit.
SIPC
A security held at a brokerage, such as a money market fund. SIPC protects you if the brokerage fails and your assets go missing (up to $500,000, including $250,000 cash). It does not protect against the fund losing value. Government money funds aim for a stable $1 share price, which is not guaranteed.
Treasury-backed
A direct loan to the US government (T-bills). There is no insurance, and none is needed in the usual sense: you are paid by the US Treasury. If you sell before maturity the price can move a little.
Not insured
Crypto stablecoin rewards (USDC). There is no FDIC or SIPC coverage. You depend on the exchange, the stablecoin issuer, and rules that are still being written.
FDIC / SIPC
A mix. For example, part of the balance is held as brokerage cash (SIPC) and the rest is swept to banks (FDIC).
