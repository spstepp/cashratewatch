CD vs high-yield savings

With a high-yield savings account you can take the money any day, but the APY can change at any time. The top savings APY this week is Western Alliance Bank High-Yield Savings via Raisin at 4.35% (Nov 24, 2026 check).

With a CD, the APY is locked for the term if you hold to maturity. Taking money out early usually costs a penalty, and at maturity the CD renews at whatever rate the bank offers then unless you choose otherwise. If a CD rate is a short promo or tied to another account, read it the same way as a promo savings rate vs the base rate. Side by side: CD vs high-yield savings.

Fixed-term bonds, Treasuries and I bonds

A fixed-term or fixed-rate bond at a bank is a CD. Treasury bills are a different product: Treasury-backed, sold at auction, and exempt from state and local income tax. See this week's T-bill auction rates and T-bills vs high-yield savings. For inflation-linked savings bonds, see I bonds vs T-bills. Matched terms: CDs vs T-bills.

Insurance

CDs at an FDIC member bank are deposits, insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category (FDIC). Brokered CDs bought through a brokerage work differently: check how they're held and what happens if you sell before maturity.

Minimums, compounding and penalties

  • Marcus: $500 minimum to open and earn the stated APY. Interest compounds daily and is credited monthly. If the rate for your term rises within 10 days of opening, you get the higher rate. Marcus charges an early withdrawal penalty; check its CD FAQs for the amount.
  • Bread Savings: $1,500 minimum in a single deposit, up to $1,000,000 per account. Early withdrawal costs 90 days of simple interest on terms under 1 year, 180 days on 12 months to 3 years, and 365 days on 4 years and up.
  • EverBank: $1,000 minimum to open. The 7-, 13- and 25-month CDs renew into 6-, 12- and 24-month CDs at maturity. Confirm the early withdrawal penalty on EverBank's CD page.

Which one fits

  • You may need the money on any day: savings or a money market fund is built for that.
  • You can leave it until a set date and want a rate that won't change: that's what a CD does.
  • You pay state income tax and can hold to a date: compare T-bill auction rates too, since T-bill interest skips state tax.

What are this week's CD APYs, and for which terms?

Bread Savings pays 3.95% to 4.85% on terms from 3 months to 5 years. Marcus pays 4.00% to 4.60% on terms from 6 months to 6 years. EverBank pays 3.40% to 4.50% on terms from 3 months to 5 years. Checked Nov 23, 2026; confirm on each bank's page before you open a CD.

Is a fixed-term bond the same thing as a CD?

In US retail banking, yes for most searches. A fixed-term or fixed-rate bond at a bank is a time deposit, which US banks call a certificate of deposit (CD). It's not a Treasury bill, an I bond or a corporate bond.

How is a CD different from a high-yield savings account?

A savings account lets you take money out any day, and its APY can change at any time. A CD's APY is fixed for the term if you hold it to maturity, and taking money out early usually costs a penalty.

Are CDs FDIC-insured?

CDs at an FDIC member bank are insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category, the same as savings. Confirm the bank's FDIC membership on its page.

What happens if I take the money out before the term ends?

Most banks charge a penalty, usually a set number of days of interest. Bread Savings charges 90 days of simple interest on terms under 1 year, 180 days on 12 months to 3 years, and 365 days on 4 years and up. Check each bank's CD page or account agreement for its exact rule.

How do CD rates compare to T-bills or I bonds this week?

They're different products. T-bills are Treasury debt sold at auction, and their interest is exempt from state and local income tax. I bonds are US savings bonds with a rate tied partly to inflation. See the T-bill rates and I bonds vs T-bills pages for those numbers.

What the risk labels mean

FDIC
A bank deposit. If the bank fails, the FDIC covers up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. Broker "cash sweep" accounts spread money across partner banks to raise that limit.
SIPC
A security held at a brokerage, such as a money market fund. SIPC protects you if the brokerage fails and your assets go missing (up to $500,000, including $250,000 cash). It does not protect against the fund losing value. Government money funds aim for a stable $1 share price, which is not guaranteed.
Treasury-backed
A direct loan to the US government (T-bills). There is no insurance, and none is needed in the usual sense: you are paid by the US Treasury. If you sell before maturity the price can move a little.
Not insured
Crypto stablecoin rewards (USDC). There is no FDIC or SIPC coverage. You depend on the exchange, the stablecoin issuer, and rules that are still being written.
FDIC / SIPC
A mix. For example, part of the balance is held as brokerage cash (SIPC) and the rest is swept to banks (FDIC).
