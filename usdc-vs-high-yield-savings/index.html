Risk: insured vs not insured

A savings account is an FDIC-insured bank deposit. USDC is a token issued by Circle and held at an exchange. It is not FDIC- or SIPC-insured. Your money depends on the exchange staying solvent and the stablecoin keeping its $1 peg. USDC briefly traded well below $1 in March 2023 when some of its reserves were stuck at Silicon Valley Bank.

Rules are still moving (GENIUS Act)

The GENIUS Act, enacted July 18, 2025, prohibits stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield to holders "solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention" of the stablecoin. Exchange rewards like Coinbase's are paid by the exchange, not directly by the issuer. On October 21, 2026 the OCC issued a final rule implementing the act, effective January 18, 2027. The rule drops a proposed presumption against certain third-party arrangements. It says a payment through an intermediary may or may not fall under the ban depending on the facts and circumstances (OCC news release; OCC Bulletin 2026-50; final rule text). Other regulators are still writing their own rules, so USDC reward rates could change or disappear.

Taxes

Savings interest is taxed as ordinary income. Crypto rewards are generally taxable income too, and you track them yourself. T-bill interest, by contrast, is exempt from state and local tax.

Bottom line

For an emergency fund, a top FDIC-insured savings account (or T-bills) gives you nearly the same yield with far less risk. USDC rewards can make sense only for money already on an exchange, if you understand that it is uninsured and that the rules may change.
